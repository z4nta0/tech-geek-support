It happens to almost everyone: an iPad that's only used now and then, a passcode that was changed and forgotten, or a grandchild who tapped in the wrong numbers a few too many times. After several wrong tries, the iPad shows a screen that says **iPad Unavailable** or **Security Lockout**.

Take a breath. You can get the iPad working again. But it's important to know one hard truth up front.

> **Before you start:** If you truly can't remember the passcode, the only way back in is to **erase the iPad** and set it up again. Apple can't unlock it for you, and no one can do it without erasing. Your photos and other information can come back afterward **only if they were backed up** to iCloud or a computer. If you're not sure, it's worth checking before you erase, for example by looking at icloud.com on a computer to see if your photos are there.

## First: did you change your passcode in the last three days?

If you changed the passcode recently and still remember the old one, you may be able to use the old one.

1. Keep entering passcodes until the **iPad Unavailable** screen appears.
2. Tap **Forgot Passcode?** near the bottom of the screen.
3. Tap **Enter Previous Passcode**.
4. Type your old passcode, then follow the steps to make a new one.

This only works within 72 hours of changing the passcode, on iPadOS 17 or later.

## Option 1: Reset the iPad by itself (no computer)

This is the easiest way for most people. You'll need:

- The iPad connected to Wi-Fi (or cellular).
- **Find My** turned on (most iPads have this on).
- Your **Apple Account** email and password. (This is the account you use for the App Store. It used to be called an Apple ID.)

Then:

1. On the lock screen, keep entering passcodes until you see **iPad Unavailable**.
2. Tap **Forgot Passcode?** at the bottom of the screen.
3. On the **Security Lockout** screen, tap **Start iPad Reset**.
4. Type your Apple Account password.
5. Tap **Erase iPad**.
6. Wait while the iPad erases and restarts. This can take several minutes.
7. Follow the setup steps on screen. When asked, choose to restore from your iCloud backup if you have one, and create a new passcode.

On older iPads (iPadOS 15.2 or 16), you'll see an **Erase iPad** button instead of **Forgot Passcode?**. It works the same way.

> **Tip:** If you don't know your Apple Account password, you can reset it first at iforgot.apple.com from any computer or phone.

## Option 2: Use a computer

If you don't see **Forgot Passcode?** (for example, the iPad isn't on Wi-Fi), you can reset it with a Mac or a Windows PC. On a Windows PC, you'll need Apple's free **Apple Devices** app from the Microsoft Store.

1. **Turn off the iPad.** Unplug it from the computer. On an iPad without a Home button, hold the top button and either volume button until the power-off slider appears. On an iPad with a Home button, hold the top button. Drag the slider, then wait a minute.
2. **Put it in recovery mode.** Connect the iPad to the computer with its cable. Then:
   - **No Home button:** press and release the volume button closest to the top button, press and release the other volume button, then press and hold the top button.
   - **Home button:** press and hold the Home button and the top button together.
   
   Keep holding until you see a picture of a computer and a cable on the iPad. Then let go.
3. On the computer, open **Finder** (Mac) or the **Apple Devices** app (Windows) and click your iPad.
4. When asked to **Restore** or **Update**, choose **Restore**.
5. Wait. If the download takes more than 15 minutes, the iPad may leave recovery mode. Let the download finish, then start again from step 1.
6. When the iPad shows **Hello**, unplug it and set it up.

## Keep this from happening again

- **Turn on iCloud Backup.** In **Settings**, tap your name, then **iCloud**, then **iCloud Backup**, and make sure it's on.
- **Write the passcode down** and keep it somewhere safe at home, like with other important papers.

## If that didn't work

- If you see a picture of a computer and cable but your computer doesn't notice the iPad, try a different cable or USB port.
- If the iPad asks for an Apple Account you don't know (for example, a used iPad), it's tied to the previous owner. Only they can remove it.
