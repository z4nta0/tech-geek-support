If your iPhone keeps showing a message that your iCloud storage is full, you're not alone. It's one of the most common iPhone worries, and it often comes with a nudge to pay for more space. You may not need to.

First, a quick explanation. **iCloud** is Apple's online storage. It keeps copies of your photos, backups, and other files on Apple's computers so they're safe if your phone is lost. Every Apple Account comes with a small amount of free iCloud space (5 GB). Photos and phone backups fill that up quickly.

## Is it iCloud, or your iPhone?

There are two different kinds of storage, and the warnings look similar:

- **iPhone storage** is the space on the phone itself.
- **iCloud storage** is your space online with Apple.

If the message says **iCloud**, keep reading. If it says **iPhone Storage**, that's a different fix (go to **Settings**, then **General**, then **iPhone Storage**).

## What happens when iCloud is full

When there's no room left, your iPhone stops backing up to iCloud, new photos stop being saved to iCloud, and an iCloud email address (one ending in @icloud.com) stops sending and receiving mail. Nothing already saved is lost, but it's worth fixing soon.

## See what's using the space

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap your name at the top.
3. Tap **iCloud**. A bar shows how full your storage is.
4. Tap **Storage** (on some versions it says **Manage Account Storage**) to see a list of what's taking up the most room.

## Let your iPhone suggest what to remove

1. In **Settings**, tap your name, then **iCloud**.
2. Tap **Recommended for You**.
3. Tap a suggestion and follow the steps on screen.

Suggestions may include large files, old backups, or photos. Read each one carefully before you agree.

## Delete old phone backups you don't need

Many people have a backup from an iPhone or iPad they no longer own. These can be large.

> **Before you start:** Only delete a backup for a device you no longer use. Deleting a backup also turns off iCloud Backup for that device.

1. In **Settings**, tap your name, then **iCloud**.
2. Tap **Storage** (or **Manage Account Storage**), then tap **Backups**.
3. Tap the name of the old device.
4. Tap **Turn Off and Delete from iCloud**, then tap **Turn Off and Delete** to confirm.

## Remove photos and videos you don't want

Photos and videos are usually the biggest space users.

> **Before you start:** When iCloud Photos is turned on, deleting a photo on your iPhone deletes it **everywhere**: on your iPad, your Mac, and in iCloud. Make sure you have a copy of anything you want to keep.

1. Open the **Photos** app.
2. Tap **Select**, then tap the photos or videos you want to remove.
3. Tap the trash can, then confirm.

Deleted items wait in the **Recently Deleted** album for 30 days in case you change your mind. One important exception: if your iCloud is already over its limit, deleted photos and videos are removed right away and can't be recovered.

> **Tip:** Videos take far more space than photos. Deleting a few long videos often frees more room than deleting hundreds of pictures.

## Should I pay for more storage?

Paying is a perfectly reasonable choice, especially if you want to keep every photo safe without sorting. Apple's paid plan is called **iCloud+**, and the smallest plan costs a small monthly fee. You can find it by tapping your name in **Settings**, then **iCloud**, and looking for the option to upgrade.

Just don't feel pushed into it. Clearing out old backups alone often solves the problem.

## If that didn't work

- **Turn off backup for apps you don't need.** In **Settings**, tap your name, **iCloud**, **Storage** (or **Manage Account Storage**), **Backups**, then your device. Turn off apps you don't need backed up and tap **Turn Off** to confirm. This removes that app's information from iCloud, so only do it for apps you're sure about.
- **Check large message attachments.** Photos and videos sent in Messages can also count toward iCloud.
