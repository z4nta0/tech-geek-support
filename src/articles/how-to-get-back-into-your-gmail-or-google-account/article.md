Getting locked out of your email is stressful, especially when your bank, photos, and family messages all depend on it. If you use Gmail, your email is part of a **Google Account**, and Google has a free, built-in way to get you back in.

There's no phone number to call and no person at Google who can unlock it for you. Instead, Google asks you some questions to confirm the account is yours. The tips below make it much more likely to work.

> **Before you start:** Google will **never** call you, text you, or email you asking for your password or a verification code. Anyone offering to "recover your Gmail" for a fee is very likely a scammer. Only type your password and codes at **accounts.google.com**.

## Set yourself up for success

Google is checking whether you look like the real owner. You can help by:

- **Using a device you usually sign in on**, like your own phone, tablet, or computer.
- **Using your usual web browser**, such as Chrome or Safari.
- **Being where you usually sign in**, like at home.

## Start the recovery

1. On your phone or computer, go to **accounts.google.com/signin/recovery**. (Or, on the Gmail sign-in page, enter your email address and click **Forgot password?**)
2. Type your Gmail address and click **Next**.
3. Google will ask a series of questions. Answer each one as best you can.
4. When Google is satisfied, you'll be asked to create a new password. Choose one you haven't used for this account before.

## How to answer the questions

- **Don't skip questions.** If you're not sure, make your best guess. Wrong guesses won't kick you out of the process.
- **"Enter the last password you remember":** type the most recent one you can recall, even if it's old. Capital letters matter.
- **Codes sent to your phone or another email:** Google may text a code to your recovery phone or email a code to your recovery email. You'll need access to that phone or inbox.
- **Can't use that option?** Choose **Try another way** to see other choices.
- **Check your spam folder** in your other email account for a message from Google called "Your Google support inquiry."

> **Tip:** If Google says it **couldn't verify that this account belongs to you**, you're allowed to try again. Try from your usual device and location if you weren't already.

## Forgot your Gmail address instead?

If you can't remember the email address itself, Google can help you find it if you know the **phone number or recovery email** on the account, plus the **full name** on the account. Choose **Forgot email?** on the sign-in page and follow the steps.

## Once you're back in: avoid this next time

Take two minutes to add backup ways for Google to reach you. These are called a **recovery phone** and a **recovery email**.

1. Go to **myaccount.google.com** and sign in.
2. Choose **Security & sign-in**.
3. Under **How you sign in to Google**, choose **Recovery phone** and follow the steps to add your mobile number.
4. Back on the same page, choose **Recovery email** and add another email address you check regularly (one that isn't this Gmail address).

Changes to your recovery phone can take up to 7 days to take full effect.

> **Tip:** Write your password down and keep it somewhere safe at home, or use a password manager (an app that remembers your passwords for you). Writing it down at home is far safer than reusing one password everywhere.

## If that didn't work

- **Wait and try again.** If you recently changed your recovery phone or email, wait up to 7 days, then try again.
- **Work or school accounts** (email addresses that don't end in @gmail.com) are often managed by your employer or school. Ask their IT helpdesk.
- **Last resort:** If recovery keeps failing, you can create a new Google Account and let your contacts know your new address.
