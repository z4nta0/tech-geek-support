Videos that keep buffering, video calls that freeze, web pages that take forever: slow Wi-Fi is frustrating, and it can be hard to tell what's wrong. The good news is that the most common fixes are free and take only a few minutes.

First, two quick terms:

- Your **modem** is the box that brings the internet into your home from your internet company.
- Your **router** is the box that turns that internet into Wi-Fi. Many internet companies give you one box that does both, often called a **gateway**.

## Step 1: Restart your router (and modem)

This really does fix a lot of problems, which is why every support line asks you to do it first.

> **Before you start:** If a light on the box is a solid orange or is flashing in an unusual way, it may be installing an update. Wait 20 minutes before unplugging it, because interrupting an update can cause problems.

1. Unplug the power cord from your router (and your modem, if it's a separate box).
2. Wait a full 60 seconds.
3. Plug the modem back in first, if you have one, and wait until its lights settle.
4. Plug the router back in.
5. Wait about 5 to 10 minutes for everything to fully start up before testing.

## Step 2: Check whether it's your Wi-Fi or your internet

A **speed test** measures how fast your internet is right now.

1. On a phone or computer, go to a speed test site such as **speedtest.net** or **fast.com**, or use your internet company's app.
2. Run the test while standing **right next to the router**.
3. Run it again in the room where things are slow.

Compare the numbers to the speed you pay for (it's on your bill).

- **Slow even next to the router?** The problem is probably with the internet service itself. Skip to "If that didn't work."
- **Fast next to the router, slow farther away?** The problem is Wi-Fi coverage in your home. The next steps will help.

## Step 3: Give your router a better spot

Wi-Fi signals get weaker with distance and with every wall and floor they pass through. Where the router sits matters a lot.

- Put it in a **central spot** in your home, not in a far corner or the basement.
- Put it **up high**, like on a shelf, not on the floor.
- Keep it **out in the open**, not inside a cabinet or behind the TV.
- Keep it away from **microwaves, cordless phone bases, and thick walls**, which can weaken the signal.

> **Tip:** If your internet comes in at one end of the house, ask your internet company whether a technician can move the connection point. It's sometimes free.

## Step 4: Cut down on what's sharing your Wi-Fi

Every connected device shares the same internet. Several people streaming video, a game console downloading an update, or a computer backing up photos can all slow things down at once.

- Pause big downloads and updates when you need the speed.
- If you think neighbors might be using your Wi-Fi, change your Wi-Fi password in your router's app. You'll need to reconnect your own devices afterward.

## Step 5: Use a cable for the most important device

If you have a TV or computer close to the router, an **Ethernet cable** (a network cable) plugged straight into the router gives the fastest, steadiest connection, and it leaves more Wi-Fi for everything else.

## If that didn't work

- **Call your internet company.** Ask whether there's an outage in your area and whether they can check the signal to your home. Have your speed test results ready.
- **Ask about your equipment.** Routers more than about five years old may not keep up with today's internet speeds. Your internet company may offer a newer model.
- **Consider a mesh system** for a larger home. A **mesh system** is a set of two or three Wi-Fi boxes placed around the house that work together as one network.
