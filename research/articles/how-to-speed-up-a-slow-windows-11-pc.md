A slow computer is one of the most common tech complaints there is, so you're in good company. The good news is that most slowdowns come from a few everyday causes: the computer hasn't been restarted in a while, too many programs open by themselves, the storage is nearly full, or updates are waiting.

Work through the fixes below in order. Check how the computer feels after each one. You may not need them all.

> **Before you start:** None of these steps delete your personal files. Still, if your photos and documents aren't backed up anywhere, now is a good time to copy them to a USB drive or to a cloud backup service such as Google Drive, Dropbox, or OneDrive.

## 1. Restart the computer (the right way)

Closing the lid or pressing the power button often just puts the computer to sleep. A real restart clears out leftover clutter.

1. Click **Start** (the Windows logo at the bottom of the screen).
2. Click **Power**.
3. Click **Restart**.

> **Tip:** Try to restart at least once a week. It fixes more problems than you might expect.

## 2. Install waiting updates

Updates fix bugs that can slow things down.

1. Click **Start**, type **settings**, then click the **Settings** app.
2. Click **Windows Update**.
3. Click **Check for updates**, and install anything it finds.
4. Restart if it asks you to.

## 3. Stop extra programs from opening at startup

Many programs set themselves to open every time Windows starts, even ones you rarely use. Each one takes a little of your computer's attention.

1. Press **Ctrl + Shift + Esc** on your keyboard (all three keys together). This opens **Task Manager**, a tool that shows what's running.
2. On the left side, click **Startup apps**.
3. Look at the list. Right-click a program you don't need right away when the computer starts, and choose **Disable**.

[Image: Task Manager open to Startup apps, with one program right-clicked and "Disable" highlighted]

Disabling doesn't remove the program. It just stops it from opening by itself. You can still open it any time you want.

> **Tip:** If you don't recognize a name, leave it alone. Leave your antivirus and anything with "audio," "touchpad," or your computer maker's name on it as they are.

## 4. Free up storage space

When the main drive (the part of the computer that stores files) is nearly full, Windows slows down.

1. Click **Start**, type **settings**, then click the **Settings** app.
2. Click **System**, then **Storage**.
3. Click **Temporary files**. Check the boxes for things you don't need, then click **Remove files**.

Be careful with the **Downloads** box. It deletes everything in your Downloads folder, so leave it unchecked unless you've looked in that folder first.

You can also turn on **Storage Sense** on the same Storage page. It cleans out temporary files for you automatically.

## 5. Remove programs you no longer use

1. Click **Start**, type **settings**, then click the **Settings** app.
2. Click **Apps**, then **Installed apps**.
3. Find a program you don't use, click the **More options** button (three dots) next to it, and click **Uninstall**.

Only remove programs you're sure about. If you don't know what something is, it's fine to leave it.

## 6. Check for viruses and other unwanted software

Unwanted software can quietly use up your computer's power. Windows 11 has free protection built in.

1. Click **Start**, type **settings**, then click the **Settings** app.
2. Click **Privacy & security**, then **Windows Security**.
3. Click **Virus & threat protection**.
4. Click **Quick scan** and let it finish.

## 7. Check the power setting

Some computers are set to save energy, which can make them feel sluggish.

1. Click **Start**, type **settings**, then click the **Settings** app.
2. Click **System**, then **Power & battery** (on desktop computers this may just say **Power**).
3. Next to **Power mode**, choose **Balanced** or **Best performance**.

**Best performance** uses more electricity, and on a laptop the battery will run down faster. **Balanced** is a good everyday choice.

## If that didn't work

- **Close extra browser tabs.** Dozens of open tabs can slow even a newer computer.
- **Consider the computer's age.** A computer that is more than six or seven years old may simply be struggling with today's websites and programs. Adding memory or replacing an old spinning hard drive with a newer type of drive can help a lot, but that's a job for a repair shop or a handy friend.
