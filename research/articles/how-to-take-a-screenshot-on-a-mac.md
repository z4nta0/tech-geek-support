A screenshot is a picture of whatever is on your screen right now. It's handy for saving a receipt, keeping a recipe, or showing someone exactly what an error message says. (It also makes it much easier for us to help you when you send a question.)

Macs don't have a "Print Screen" key like many Windows keyboards. Instead, you press a few keys at the same time. Here are the three you'll use most.

## The keys you'll need

All three shortcuts use the same two keys plus a number:

- **Shift** (the key with an upward arrow, above the Control key)
- **Command** (the key with the ⌘ symbol, right next to the space bar)

Press and hold **Shift** and **Command** together, then press the number. Let go of all the keys afterward.

[Image: A Mac keyboard with Shift, Command, and the 3, 4, and 5 keys highlighted]

## Take a picture of the whole screen

1. Press **Shift + Command + 3**.
2. You may hear a camera shutter sound. That's it. The picture is saved.

## Take a picture of just part of the screen

1. Press **Shift + Command + 4**. Your pointer turns into a small crosshair (a plus sign).
2. Click and drag a box around the part you want.
3. Let go of the mouse or trackpad. The picture is saved.

Changed your mind? Press the **Esc** key (top-left of the keyboard) before you let go to cancel.

## Take a picture of one window

1. Press **Shift + Command + 4**.
2. Press the **space bar** once. The pointer turns into a small camera.
3. Move the camera over the window you want. It will turn light blue.
4. Click. The picture is saved.

> **Tip:** Hold down the **Option** key when you click if you'd like the picture without the shadow around the window's edge.

## Use the Screenshot toolbar (more choices)

If you'd rather click buttons than remember shortcuts, press **Shift + Command + 5**. A small toolbar appears at the bottom of the screen with buttons for the whole screen, one window, or a selected area. It can also record a video of your screen.

The **Options** menu on that toolbar lets you:

- Choose where screenshots are saved.
- Set a timer (5 or 10 seconds) so you have time to open a menu first.
- Turn the little preview picture on or off.

[Image: The Screenshot toolbar at the bottom of the screen with the Options menu open]

## Where do my screenshots go?

Unless you change it, every screenshot is saved on your **desktop** (the main screen behind your windows). The file name includes the date and time it was taken, so the newest one is easy to spot.

Right after you take one, a small preview picture appears in the bottom-right corner for a few seconds:

- Click it to mark it up, crop it, or share it.
- Ignore it and it saves itself.
- Drag it straight into an email or a folder if you like.

## Copy instead of save

Want to paste the screenshot straight into an email or message without saving a file? Add the **Control** key to any shortcut. For example, **Control + Shift + Command + 4** copies a selected area. Then click where you want it and choose **Edit**, then **Paste**.

> **Tip:** Some apps, such as the Apple TV app, block screenshots of their window to protect movies and shows. If you get a blank picture, that's why.

## If that didn't work

- **Nothing happened?** Make sure you're pressing **Command** (⌘) and not **Option** or **Control**. Hold the first two keys down first, then press the number.
- **Can't find the file?** Press **Shift + Command + 5**, click **Options**, and look at the checked place under **Save to**. That's where your screenshots are going.
